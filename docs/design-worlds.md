# Three businesses, three reasons to care

These are fictional teaching directions. No customer reaction, owner approval or local-business fact is implied. Use them to learn the decision, not to stamp the same aesthetic onto another client. The runnable examples in `examples/` demonstrate restrained typographic versions; the richer image and interaction treatments below are optional build directions, not features already implemented.

| Choice | Neighbourhood restaurant | Appointment barber | Local repairer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Visitor situation | Looking for somewhere to settle into a meal | Wants to feel comfortable with the person cutting their hair | Needs to know whether this business handles the problem and area |
| Intended experience | Welcome, appetite, a reason to linger | Recognition, ease and attention | Relief, clarity and trust |
| Protected idea | A meal makes room for people | Time and care are visible in the craft | The problem becomes understandable |
| Visual grammar | Broad curves, generous type, a warm opening and quieter practical details | Precise type, strong contrast, open space around comparable services | Compact opening, a coverage panel and a legible work list |
| Rhythm | Invitation → permitted food image if supplied → menu → practical visit → enquiry | Name/point of view → services → visit expectations → appointment enquiry | Problem/contact → coverage → services → enquiry |
| Scarce-photo route | Original serving-vessel illustration family; no fabricated dishes or diners | Original comb/mirror silhouettes with consistent stroke; no synthetic portfolio | Simple diagnostic diagrams only if accurate; no invented before/after work |
| Earned play | Optional occasion choice helps someone draft an enquiry | A restrained service reveal explains inclusions | Often none: a quick answer is enough |
| Delete | Repeated category labels, cart totals with no fulfilment | Generic luxury badges, fictional testimonials | Decorative delay, unverified “24/7” and instant-price claims |
| Reality check | Can a visitor tell what is offered and how to visit? | Do they understand what they are asking to book? | Can they establish fit and reach the correct contact immediately? |

## Equivalent intent, different expression

The following lines are **draft exercises requiring fluent review**, not universal replacement copy. Service details must explain the actual offer nearby.

| Situation | Català | Español de España | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Restaurant welcome | Hi ha lloc per a una bona estona. | Hay sitio para un buen rato. | Make time for a good meal. |
| Barber invitation | Un tall que vagi amb tu. | Un corte que vaya contigo. | A cut that feels like you. |
| Repair enquiry | Explica'ns què passa. | Cuéntanos qué pasa. | Tell us what needs fixing. |

## One useful exercise

Choose one practice sector and ask the copilot: “Show me the same offer with a different visual mechanism, preserving the facts and the customer action.” Save the current version first. Change the opening composition or one shape family, not every variable. Compare phone screenshots and the customer task. Keep the version that better expresses the intended feeling while remaining clear. A classmate's preference is useful feedback, not a measured increase in business enquiries.
