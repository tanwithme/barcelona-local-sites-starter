# From instructions to a working conversation

This revision used the current project conversation, existing personal writing guidance and a targeted sample of twelve excerpts from Tan-labelled speaking turns in four July 2026 work conversations. The recurring situations were teaching AI use, questioning a proposed workflow, explaining copy direction and helping someone make reusable design guidance. Guest speech was excluded as voice evidence. Two additional craft/language transcripts had no reliable Tan-labelled turns and were excluded. This is not an exhaustive Granola audit or an analysis of every register.

| Observed move in the eligible sample | Change in this pack | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start with the person's actual work and respect their time | Open with a useful example; explain only the choice that helps now | Do not assume every learner is anxious or needs a lesson |
| Ask what people are feeling or trying to do before generating more material | Listen for a real customer question before choosing a section or feature | A small sample of comments is not an audience study |
| Make an abstract point tangible with a specific example | Show the sentence, interaction or comparison the recommendation changes | A fluent explanation is not proof of a better outcome |
| Carry good examples and corrections into reusable guidance | Preserve the client's voice and record local corrections | One accepted example is not a universal brand rule |
| Adapt the means when a tool is unavailable | Keep the experience and make a supported draft | Never claim access, delivery or testing that did not happen |

The former copy was clear but often sounded like a checklist. The revised welcome, coaching instructions and fictional website examples keep the practical facts while making the relationship easier to enter: a situation, a reason, something to try. Repeated generic service placeholders now ask for the information particular to that service.

The public guide contains original adaptations only. No raw transcripts, private meeting titles, participant names, internal client examples or source-file identifiers are distributed. Detailed source anchors remain outside the repository. Spanish and Catalan are adaptations of the observed thinking moves, not a claim that the source established Tan's native written voice in those languages.

Read [the voice guide](studio-voice.md). Keep studio coaching separate from the client's own public voice. The next useful check is a real beginner using the welcome and a competent reader reviewing each language; this revision does not claim either acceptance.
